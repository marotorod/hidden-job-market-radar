# Hiring signals

A hiring signal is an event that makes a company likely to hire for the candidate's kind of role before an opening is published. Finding one early lets the candidate reach the company through a warm introduction or a recruiter while the role is still being defined.

## Signals and what they usually mean

| Signal | Roles it often creates | Strength |
|---|---|---|
| Growth funding round (Series B or later, private equity investment) | Commercial, operations and finance leadership to scale | High |
| New CEO or new business-unit head | The new leader's own team, usually within 3 to 9 months | High |
| Senior departure (a peer of the target role leaves) | Direct replacement | High |
| Expansion: new market, country, office or plant | Country manager, regional and operations leadership | High |
| Merger or acquisition | Integration roles; duplicated roles at risk | Medium |
| IPO preparation | CFO-office, investor relations, governance, finance systems | Medium |
| New product line or regulatory change | Product, compliance and go-to-market leadership | Medium |
| Restructuring or layoffs at a competitor | Talent available for the competitor's rivals; sometimes the competitor rebuilds later | Low to medium |
| A cluster of junior or mid-level openings in one function | A leader for that function is likely next | Medium |

## Where to find them

Use web search and the company's own channels. Don't rely on paid databases or APIs the environment can't reach.

- The company's newsroom and press releases, investor relations page and annual report.
- Sector and business press, and newsletters in the candidate's sector.
- Official registries and filings where they apply (for example company registries, stock-market regulators and public tenders).
- Leadership announcements on LinkedIn: public posts only, read through search results or a connector.
- Funding announcements reported by the press or the investors' own sites.

Useful searches:

```text
"{company}" (raises OR "funding round" OR "Series B" OR "Series C")
"{company}" (appoints OR "joins as" OR "new CEO" OR "chief executive")
"{company}" (expansion OR "new office" OR "enters the" OR "launches in")
"{sector}" "{country}" (raises OR acquisition OR "new CEO") after:{date}
```

## How to use a signal

1. Verify it in a source you retrieved, and record the source and date. A rumour is not a signal.
2. Log it in `recruiters_log.md` as **MARKET SIGNAL**, with the company, the event, the date and the role it may create.
3. If the company fits the candidate's universe, check `contacts.csv` for someone there and draft a warm-introduction request (`network_and_referrals.md`). Otherwise, check which executive search firm works with the company.
4. Report it in section 5 of the report, with what it may mean and the suggested next step.
5. A signal at a company outside the universe can justify adding it as DISCOVERY.
